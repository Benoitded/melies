# Competitors: what users say (forums and reviews, 2026-10-22)

Report from a research subagent, reviewed and condensed.

## Limits to keep in mind

- **Reddit could not be read** (the reading tools are refused). Everything labeled Reddit
  comes secondhand (a competitor blog or AlternativeTo quoting it). X and Bluesky: unreachable.
- Actually read: Hacker News (about 5 threads read in detail), Cap's Product Hunt reviews (8 reviews,
  3.9 out of 5), CleanShot X's Setapp reviews (about 10), the Screen Studio feedback forum, the
  AlternativeTo news.
- Small sample: "N sources" means "N sources that mention it", not a statistic.
  Hacker News commenters are mostly developers and price-sensitive.

## By product

| Product | What people like | What people complain about | Why people leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen Studio | polished demos, smoothed cursor, auto zoom; "worth it" when it was a one-time purchase with one year of updates | lifetime license at $89, then $229 (plus $109 per year), then **subscription only** ($9/month billed annually, $29/month monthly); crashes; Mac only; videos that do not play in Safari; requests: reliable cropping, speed, cursor size, arrows, zoom that can be turned off, blur that follows the content | the switch to a subscription; they cite Cap, Screenity, Canvid, OpenScreen, OBS |
| CleanShot X | all-in-one, easy, scrolling capture, daily recording | **breaks after updates** (slow on Intel after 5.0.1, does not load on macOS 27), GIF quality, hidden features, a Trustpilot review about an upload to a personal server removed without notice, update fee fatigue | cost and update fees |
| Shottr | OCR, speed, lightness, annotation (8 comments and more) | no video or GIF; annotation tools simpler than CleanShot's | need for video, hence CleanShot |
| Loom | instant link, comments, analytics, transcript-based editing | poor free tier (5 min), business practices seen as aggressive, unreliable Chrome tab capture | price, free limits, wish for self-hosting |
| Cap | instant link, good zoom effects for the price, open source, fast support | crashes and launch failures, multi-display recording that does not start, slow exports in Studio mode, screenshot editor needs polish; "beta version" according to the founder | reliability (one user went back to Screen Studio) |
| Kap | no direct feedback found | maintenance not verified | n/a |
| Recordly | free, auto zoom, smoothed cursor, motion blur, backgrounds, GIF | no key display, little audio control | n/a |
| QuickTime / Cmd+Shift+5 | free, basic reference | no link, no effects, basic trim, **huge files** (about 105 MB for a full screen, 10 times more than OBS), no system sound | n/a |
| OBS | free, powerful | learning curve, built for live streaming, no auto zoom or editor | people move to something simpler |

## Qualities cited as "must-haves", by number of sources

1. Honest price, no subscription (one-time purchase with a paid update option): about 6.
2. Reliability, no crashes, including after a macOS update: about 5.
3. Polished auto zoom and smoothed cursor: about 5.
4. Local first, privacy, no forced cloud: about 4.
5. Basic editing (trim, speed, annotations in the video): about 4.
6. **Small exports with a target size**: about 3. A Screen Studio user asked for a maximum
   export size (for example 15 MB for Twitter); the request got 2 votes and was
   **rejected**.
7. Instant sharing and dragging the file elsewhere: about 3.
8. System sound and mic: about 2.
9. Key display, fine audio control: 1 (anecdote).
10. Native and light (not Electron): 1 to 2.

## Price: what is accepted or hated

Accepted: one-time purchase with one year of updates and optional renewal; small one-time prices
(Shottr about $12, ScreenSnap Pro $29 or $39, Debut $25, Screenium $59.99).
Hated: monthly subscription for a desktop app (especially $29/month); forced updates;
changed license that removes features from existing buyers (CleanShot, Screen Studio);
free tools that add reminders (Shottr, according to a competitor, not verified).

## The gaps

- A **native** app with polished auto zoom and **a maximum export size**: Screen Studio refused.
- "Record, trim, annotate, export small, drag to Twitter or Slack" in one gesture, with no
  cloud account. CleanShot comes close but breaks after updates; Shottr has no video.
- **Annotations with a duration in the video** (a CleanShot user asks for them).
- Masking that follows moving content.
- Honest compatibility with macOS updates.

## What the study suggests for Melies

1. Sell as a one-time purchase with one year of updates, never as a subscription alone.
2. Make reliability the #1 argument: test every macOS beta.
3. **Export to a target size** (for example 15 MB) with a Twitter preset.
4. Local first, no account.
5. Auto zoom with manual control, fine zoom slider, can be turned off.
6. Annotations with a duration (arrow, text, spotlight, blur).
7. Reliable trim and speed from the start.
8. Drag-and-drop of the exported file and fast results.
9. Simple mic and system sound, 3-2-1 countdown.
10. To avoid: heavy Electron, hidden interface, removing features from buyers, insistent
    purchase reminders. Keep GIF honest (the quality of CleanShot's GIFs is criticized).
